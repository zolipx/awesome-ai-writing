Studios build sets so they can control every little thing: the look, the sounds, the mood. If the story lives in a made-up world or needs a very specific feeling, a set lets them make it exactly right.

On-location filming can look cheap at first. Renting a place can get pricey, especially if the crew needs it for a long time, and they may also have to pay to use certain spots and to change them so the scene works.

Real places are tricky to shoot in. There can be crowds, noise, and distractions that ruin takes. The crew might also miss basics they need there, like steady power, water, or special gear.

Safety matters. Streets, shaky ground, and bad weather can hurt people or stop work. A set is a controlled space, so those hazards are kept small.

Building sets can cost more upfront. Over time it can still save money, because the crew controls the space and avoids delays or big surprise bills later.
