Movie teams often build sets instead of using real places because:

- Control: On a set, they can make everything look exactly how they want. That helps for made-up worlds or when they need a very specific mood or feel.

- Cost: Real locations aren’t always cheaper. Renting a place can cost a lot, especially for a long time. They might have to pay just to use it, and also pay to change it so it fits the scene.

- Logistics: Real places can be hard to work in. There can be crowds, noise, and other distractions. They also might not have what the crew needs there, like enough electricity, water, or special equipment.

- Safety: Real locations can be risky, with things like traffic, uneven ground, or bad weather. A set is a controlled place where those risks are smaller.

Overall, sets can cost more at the start, but they can be cheaper in the long run because the crew has more control, which helps avoid delays and surprise costs.
