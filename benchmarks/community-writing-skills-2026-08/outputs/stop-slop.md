Studios build sets for simple, practical reasons:

- Control: On a set, they decide how everything looks and feels. This matters when the story lives in a make‑believe or fictional world, or when they want a specific mood or atmosphere.

- Cost: A real place can look cheaper, but not always. Renting a place can cost a lot, and costs grow if they need it for a long time. They might have to pay just to use the spot, and also pay to change it to fit the story.

- Logistics: Real places bring crowds, noise, and other distractions. The team might not get what they need there, like electricity, water, or special equipment.

- Safety: Sets can keep people safer. Real places can bring traffic, shaky ground, or bad weather. On a set, the team controls the space and lowers those hazards.

Sets can cost more at the start, but over time they can save money because the team controls the space, avoids delays, and cuts the risk of surprise costs.
